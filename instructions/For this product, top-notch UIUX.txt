For this product, top-notch UI/UX would come from **hiding the enormous technical complexity without removing professional control**.

The wrong approach would be to put 50 models, 300 parameters, RAG, agents, editing, music, billing, assets and fine-tuning into menus and expect the user to understand the system.

The right approach is:

> **Simple when you enter. Powerful when you dig deeper. Context-aware everywhere. Nothing gets lost. AI is reversible. Cost is visible.**

That direction is increasingly visible in current creative tools. Runway has moved toward an Agent-first entry point with projects, sessions, tools and workflows behind it; ElevenLabs' newly released Studio 4.0 generates video/image/voice/music/SFX directly into a timeline; and its Studio Agent works alongside manual editing rather than replacing it. ([Runway Help][1])

```text
│ V3 ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────  │

│ V1 ─■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■■  │
```

The user learns the layout once.

The **center changes**, but the basic mental model doesn't.

Story Room? Center becomes screenplay.

Image Lab? Center becomes canvas.

Cinema? Center becomes shot/storyboard viewer.

Music? Center becomes DAW.

Video Editor? Center becomes program monitor.

The right inspector and bottom timeline remain familiar.

ElevenLabs is already using this contextual-sidebar pattern in Studio: the right panel changes according to the selected narration/audio object, while its timeline remains the project-wide structural view. ([ElevenLabs][2])

# 2. Three UI depths: Quick / Director / Pro

This may be one of the most important UX decisions.

### Quick

A beginner sees:

```text
What do you want to create?

[ A cinematic perfume commercial...                  ]

Reference       + Add
Aspect ratio    16:9
Duration        15 sec

                   Generate
```

No sampler.

No CFG.

No model endpoint.

No confusing settings.

---

### Director

Click:

**Director Controls**

and suddenly:

```text
Character      Sarah
Location       Paris Apartment
Camera         ARRI-style
Lens           50mm
Aperture       f/2
Movement       Slow dolly in
Lighting       Window / golden hour
Mood           Melancholic
Tempo          Slow
Continuity     Locked
```

This is approximately the design insight Higgsfield describes behind Cinema Studio: they deliberately reduced a huge cinematography vocabulary to a smaller group of controls that creators can understand, then made camera/lens choices visually meaningful. ([Higgsfield AI][3])

---

### Pro

Click:

```text
Advanced ▾
```

and expose:

```text
Provider
Model
Model version
Seed
Reference weights
Start frame
End frame
Resolution
FPS
Motion strength
Provider-specific parameters
Custom model
LoRA
Generation count
Safety parameters
API routing
```

Nothing gets converted when switching levels.

Quick, Director and Pro are just different views of the **same underlying generation specification**.

That is critical.

Instead of:

```text

```

I would show:

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ Generate 4 takes          $1.84 │
│ ~45–70 sec                       │
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

Click the arrow:

```text

Generate 2          ~$0.92
Generate 4          ~$1.84

Draft quality       ~$0.41
Production           ~$1.84
Hero quality         ~$4.20
```

This creates enormous trust.

The creator always understands:

**what will happen + how long-ish the operation is + what it costs.**

# 5. AI should manipulate the UI, not trap users in chat

This is a major distinction.

Bad AI UX:

```text
USER:
Make the sequence more dramatic.

AI:
Here is your new video.
```

Better:

```text
USER:
Make the sequence more dramatic.
```

Then the AI proposes:

```text
PROPOSED CHANGES

Scene 12
+ shorten Shot 12A by 0.8s
+ replace Shot 12C with close-up
+ reduce score 4dB before reveal
+ add 12-frame pause
+ generate reaction shot

Estimated generation cost: $0.82

[Preview]
[Apply]
```

Everything becomes **normal editable operations**.

ElevenLabs' current Studio Agent follows a related direction: it can construct a timeline conversationally, but the creator can immediately take over manually and then hand control back to the agent. ([ElevenLabs][4])

That's exactly the interaction philosophy I would extend throughout StudioOS.

A feature film might contain:


Runway currently organizes assets around projects, sessions, folders, recents and favorites; sessions specifically group related generations for comparison/iteration. ([Runway Help][5])

# 11. Never throw away generations


# 40. The overall UX philosophy

I would define the product internally with ten laws:

1. **The project is always the context.**
2. **Nothing generated gets lost.**
3. **AI actions are editable and reversible.**
4. **Simple controls appear before technical controls.**
5. **Users think in shots, scenes, characters, products and music—not API endpoints.**
6. **Model selection is automatic unless the creator wants control.**
7. **Generation happens where the asset will be used.**
8. **Cost is known before expensive actions.**
9. **Everything can be found through search or ⌘K.**
10. **Manual and AI workflows are never separate worlds.**

That last point may matter the most.

The emerging direction in current creative products supports it: ElevenLabs' newest Studio brings multiple generation modalities directly into a common timeline, its Agent deliberately coexists with manual editing, Runway organizes agent/tool/workflow interactions inside project context, and Higgsfield has focused its filmmaking UI on human cinematography concepts rather than exposing raw model complexity. ([ElevenLabs][7])

If we execute that well, **StudioOS shouldn't feel like “20 AI tools integrated into a dashboard.”**

It should feel more like:

> **a filmmaking/commercial-production environment that simply happens to have dozens of AI models working underneath it.**

That's the difference between an impressive AI demo and a product professionals could live in for eight hours a day.
